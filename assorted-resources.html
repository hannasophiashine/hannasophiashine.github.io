Grad School & Applications

Also, a reminder that I'm happy to chat about lab manager or PhD applications and give feedback on materials if I can (see my home page)!

General advice

  • The Portable Mentor — a guide/handbook for applying to graduate school + early grad student/faculty advice. Honestly, this feels very clinical-focused, but some of the more general advice is helpful! Time management skills!! Important! & this is free :)

Free application mentorship programs

  • PPREP (Prospective Ph.D. & RA Event in Psychology) — a free, virtual weekend of panels and application workshops run by the Harvard Psychology department, followed by one-on-one feedback on your materials (personal statement or cover letter, plus CV) from a faculty member, postdoc, or grad student in your area. It's for people applying to PhD programs or RA/lab manager positions anywhere, not just at Harvard, and it prioritizes applicants from economically or resource-disadvantaged backgrounds. Applications are due in the fall. Would highly recommend, Harvard faculty are wonderful folks!
  • MIT BCS Application Assistance Program (AAP) — current MIT Brain & Cognitive Sciences grad students answer questions about grad school, student life, and application materials. Sign-ups open in the fall, and you don't have to apply to MIT to use it!
  • MIT Graduate Application Assistance Programs — the full list of MIT's student-run application help programs, in case you're applying in other fields (e.g., linguistics-adjacent or computational stuff).
  • Lots of other departments run similar programs (Harvard's PPREP page mentions Stanford, Michigan, and Yale, for example), so it's worth searching "[department] application assistance program" for the places you're applying!

Fellowships

  • Alex Lang's NSF GRFP page — a big collection of example essays from past NSF GRFP winners, plus general advice. Heads up: the essay format has changed over the years, so check when an example is from!

Logic!

Most of these come from the course materials page for Dr. Richard Heck's PHIL 0640 at Brown. All credit goes to Riki for compiling them! I literally just copy and pasted them here. BTW, logic notation & terminology aren't fully standardized, so symbols & such may look a bit different from source to source.

Logic puzzles (for fun!)

  • Nicole's Book of Logic Puzzles — by Brown graduate Nicole Fegan, who also shares puzzles on her Substack.
  • Clues By Sam — another source of logic puzzles.

Free online textbooks

  • forall x — by P. D. Magnus
  • forall x: Calgary — an expanded, updated version of forall x!
  • Symbolic Logic: A First Course — by Gary Hardegree
  • An Introduction to Formal Logic (2nd ed.) — by Peter Smith (exercises and solutions only)
  • The Open Logic Project — a free, open-source collection of logic textbooks, has things about metalogic and modal logic
  • Invitation to Formal Semantics — by Elizabeth Coppock & Lucas Champollion, available on Elizabeth Coppock's website. The vibe here is using logic as a tool for natural language meaning, if you're coming from a ling background this is a good one!!
  • Beginning Mathematical Logic study guide — by Peter Smith, you conqured intro logic, now what...!

Interactive practice stuff

  • Carnap — do logic exercises (proofs, truth tables, translations, etc.) with automatic checking; built around forall x: Calgary

Reference

  • Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Classical Logic — P.S. any topics in the realm of phil, ling, cog sci etc. are great to look up on SEP!
